Ethical reliability of AI advisory systems

Expert validation study · Universidad Pablo de Olavide
This survey is closed. The first-wave expert validation is no longer accepting responses. Thank you to everyone who took part — your ratings have been analysed and reported.

What this was

Between May 2025 and 2026, a panel of experts rated 50 adaptive dialogues in which five frontier language models were asked for business advice on ethically charged decisions and then pushed back on over four further turns. Their ratings served as the human reference standard against which an automated scoring pipeline was validated.

The results are now published:

Chaves-Maza, M. (2026). Should Businesses Trust AI Advice? A Methodology to Audit the Ethical Integrity of Chatbots. Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 101291. 10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101291

The study continues

A second wave re-collected the dialogues fourteen months later with the current reasoning generation of the same five systems, and added a condition in which every model was given an identical, fully specified business context. It asks whether the first wave's ranking survives model turnover, and whether the differences between systems are an artefact of the context each model had to guess at.

That second-wave validation is open by invitation. If you have been invited, the form is at upo.digital/ethicresearch/survey.html. If you would like to take part and have not been invited, write to mchaves@upo.es.
